The Cup at Pine Dunes

Course Notes

Pine Dunes Resort & Golf Club · Par 72
← back to Team HQ
Overall: The course is great and rewards good golf shots. It's pretty tight around the whole place, so if you get in the trees, don't be a hero. Just play smart and get it back in play.

Front Nine Par 36
1Hole 1 Par 4376 / 391 ydsHcp 11

Tight dogleg left with a bunker on the right side. Hit something that keeps you short of that bunker as we want to take that out of play. It will most likely be a hybrid, 3-wood, or driver for Dylan off this first tee. Should leave a short iron or wedge downhill to a green that slopes back to front. Do not miss long.

2Hole 2 Par 4314 ydsHcp 17

Short, dead straight par 4. Dealer's choice off the tee — lay up to a comfy yardage or just bang driver. The further you hit it off the tee, the wider it gets down the right side. Green is perched up a little bit with some decent undulation. Take note of where that pin is for the day.

3Hole 3 Par 3130 ydsHcp 15

Kind of a blind tee shot to this par 3. It's short but you must be accurate as the green falls off the planet on the left side. Anything left of the middle of the green has an opportunity to hit the slope on the left and miss the green. It plays a touch uphill.

4Hole 4 Par 4432 ydsHcp 1

Hard dogleg left. It's most likely 200–210 through the fairway if you hit it straight, which leaves another 200-yard shot in — not a bad strategy here because it's the number 1 handicap hole. I'd rather guys lay up off the tee to get in play versus slinging a big hook around the corner. Hitting a 2nd shot within 50 yards of the green is an advantage for guys that try to hit that hook and end up in the trees. Play smart golf and understand your stroke holes in the match. Around the green is pretty simple — miss is short.

5Hole 5 Par 5469 ydsHcp 9

Intimidating tee shot if you aren't familiar with this one. A large bunker runs down the left (OB is directly left of the bunker). If you can carry the bunker, just smash it over — about a 250 carry. If not, hit something down the right (tons of trees in the middle of the fairway). If you lay up, the right side of the fairway is better because you'll get a better look into the green. You have a backstop on the back side of the green. Good hole to try and take advantage of.

6Hole 6 Par 3225 ydsHcp 3

Long but wide par 3. It plays a touch downhill. Missing it short in the fairway is not a bad place to be at all — it leaves a straightforward chip up the green. Don't miss this left because the bunkers are deep and hard to get out of.

7Hole 7 Par 4409 ydsHcp 5

Another tough/blindish tee shot where the hole turns a touch left. Pick a line you're comfy with and bang driver. You'll likely have a mid-short iron into a green heavily surrounded by trees. The green itself is fairly flat.

8Hole 8 Par 4360 ydsHcp 13

Short, tight hole with a bunker and a tree down the left side. This hole moves left too, and the 2nd shot plays uphill. Best to lay up off this tee with a hybrid or something that goes 200–220, leaving a mid-short iron for the uphill 2nd shot. Junk is left of the green — so just hit the green or miss it short.

9Hole 9 Par 5512 ydsHcp 7

Fairly straight hole that then turns left slightly. Bunkers on the left you want to avoid because they're fairly deep. Bang driver down the right side, which leaves you a long way in. Play this as a 3-shot hole — the 2nd shot is not easy either, with a really long bunker down the left side to avoid. If you hit a good layup, the 3rd shot is easier as it's a wide/flat green. Two-putt par and go to the back.

Back Nine Par 36
10Hole 10 Par 4403 ydsHcp 8

Gentle dogleg right with 2 bunkers that pinch in at 240 (right bunker) to 280 (left bunker). Missing right is dead. You can miss left, but the hole gets a lot longer. Ideal play is something 240 off the tee that leaves you short of all the bunkers and maximizes your landing zone. Your approach will be 145–175 yards depending on the pin. Hitting to this green, just like the tee shot, right is very bad — deep bunker, and anything right of the green bounds down a hill. Treat this like a very easy par 5 rather than a tough par 4 if you want to take a big number out of play — especially if you're getting a stroke. Hit your second about 50 yards short and leave yourself a very easy pitch.

11Hole 11 Par 5575 ydsHcp 4

Funky par 5 with a lot of sand. Play off the tee is always to aim at the left bunker and hit it about 240. Keep your layup number short of the right bunker and you'll have a very large landing zone. The right greenside bunker extends almost to the center of the green (depth), so keep all your third shots a touch left and long. If you get into trouble off the tee or after your layup, don't panic — a lot of things can happen here. There are a lot of ways your opponent can double this hole, so even if you get in trouble, focus on getting the ball safely back in play and let your opponent make a mistake.

12Hole 12 Par 3164 ydsHcp 12

Uphill par 3. Don't miss right (starting to see a pattern!) — there's a huge hill and you'll be playing your pitch blind, about 20 ft below the pin. Center-left is the safest spot to aim. Par on this hole is a good score.

13Hole 13 Par 4395 ydsHcp 10

Slight dogleg left. Bunkers on the right make you think you can't miss right, but actually it's short left that is dead (lost ball in the natural area). Keep it in play and you'll have a relatively easy iron into a big green. If you end up in one of those right bunkers, just get back in play and try to get up and down for par. Aim for the center of the green and you'll have a relatively easy 2-putt no matter the pin.

14Hole 14 Par 4394 ydsHcp 6

This straightaway par 4 has water on the right. The fairway slopes right and the ball can bound into the water even if it lands in the fairway — so keep the ball left-center. Unfortunately there's a tree protecting the left side of the green, so a safe tee shot often makes the approach very difficult. Don't bite off more than you can chew — play to the safe side even if that's away from the pin and misses the green right. If you walk away with par here, you did something right.

15Hole 15 Par 4311 ydsHcp 16

Short but tough par 4 — this might be your toughest tee shot of the day. You can't miss right (water) and you can't miss left (pitching out). If you have a 190-yard straight shot, this is the hole to use it. The bunkers at the end of the fairway can be tough, but they're infinitely better than the water, which almost always leaves a 200+ shot after the drop. (And there's a good chance the ball gets caught up in the rough short of the bunker and leaves you under 100 yards in.)

16Hole 16 Par 3154 ydsHcp 18

Make sure you take enough club on this mid-length par 3. The only difficult pin is back-left, which slopes away from you and can be hard to stop if it lands on the downslope. Play to the center-back of this green and you'll never leave disappointed.

17Hole 17 Par 4434 ydsHcp 2

A monster of a hole, dogleg right. This hole practically invites you to make a dumb decision… so don't. You'll want to take on the dogleg — don't. You'll want to swing hard to get inside 200 for your second — don't. I bet this is the hardest hole on the course, with the most lost balls, and it'll play hard because people lose their minds and play bad strategy. Play the ball to the left-center of the dogleg and hit something 200–230 off the tee. Suck it up and have a stupidly long second shot — play to the front or even short of the green (probably another 200–230). If you manage a par, brag about it after drinks.

18Hole 18 Par 5480 ydsHcp 14

Very easy finishing hole. Play it safe and you should have a very easy par: hit it 190 off the tee, lay up to 100, and make sure you give yourself enough club for the uphill 3rd shot. If you want to take on some risk and can carry driver 250, go right over the water and leave yourself 130 in. Just check the wind before you hit your 2nd/3rd shot up the hill to the tiered green.
